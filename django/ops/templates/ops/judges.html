Try TapFlash in five minutes

In Nairobi, a clean public restroom that is open when you need it is hard to find, and the people who keep restrooms clean are often paid late, in cash. TapFlash is a WhatsApp bot that finds the restroom, takes the payment with PayPal, refunds failed visits, and pays operators and cleaners as soon as their work is approved.

Everything below runs live on this server. Do the four stops in order. Stop 1 is two separate visits, because one payment can only end one way: either the door opens and the hold is charged, or the visit fails and the hold is released.

Two visits: one works, one fails

Visit 1: the door opens.

  1. In the chat, type Niko Kencom, nataka choo safi: Swahili for "I'm at Kencom, I want a clean toilet". Gemini understands and answers in Swahili.
  2. Pick a restroom, then Pay and PayPal. Log in with the sandbox buyer above. PayPal holds the money; it is not charged yet.
  3. The receipt, the walking route and the access QR code arrive in the chat.
  4. Open the door check (stop 2) and scan that QR code, or type its 6-digit code. Press Let them in: PayPal now takes the held payment.
  5. Scan the same code again: it says Already used.

Visit 2: the door is locked.

  1. Start a new visit and pay again, the same way. Do not scan this one.
  2. Write The door was locked. Gemini reviews it and the code releases the hold: you are never charged.
  3. Why it matters

In January 2026 the US CDC reported that public toilets in Kenya that are not cleaned well spread diarrhoeal illness and worsen cholera and mpox outbreaks. It found high turnover and low morale among cleaners, and skipped disinfection, and what worked was checking the work and giving feedback (CDC). Where private operators ran Nairobi's public toilets, each served 500 to 1,000 visitors a day (World Bank WSP).

In Kampala, a busy public toilet takes up to Shs120,000 on a good day, in cash and with no record (Daily Monitor). TapFlash turns that into PayPal payments with a record an operator can show a bank.

TapFlash checks every cleaning with a photo, pays the cleaner at once with PayPal, and shows the next visitor that the restroom was just cleaned.
